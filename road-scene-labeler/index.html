      Error types

      Labeling rules for this project. Every reviewed scene is scored against them.

      1. Tight boxes. Box edges touch the outermost pixels of the object, with no extra background.
      2. Vehicle. Cars, SUVs and trucks. Include wheels and the full body.
      3. Pedestrian. Anyone on foot, on the sidewalk or crossing the road.
      4. Cyclist. One box around the rider and the bicycle together. Do not add a separate Pedestrian box for the rider.
      5. Traffic light. Box the light housing only, not the pole or the arm.
      6. Stop sign. Box the red octagon only. Speed limit and other signs are not labeled.
      7. Occluded. When something nearer hides part of an object, box where the whole object would be and tick Occluded.
      8. Truncated. When an object runs off the edge of the image, box it up to the edge and tick Truncated.
      9. Optional objects. Very small or almost fully hidden objects are optional. Labeling them is fine. Skipping them costs nothing.
      How scoring works

      IoU (intersection over union) measures how well two boxes overlap: the shared area divided by the combined area. A box counts as correct when its IoU with the answer key box is at least 0.50 and the class matches. Boxes at 0.70 or higher count as tight.

      Precision is the share of your boxes that were correct. Recall is the share of required objects you found. The quality score blends both (70%) with box tightness (30%).

      Built in real photos (COCO)

      The Real photos set holds 10 street photos from the COCO dataset, each labeled by human annotators. Boxes cover the visible part of each object. COCO has no Occluded flag, so only Truncated is checked on these photos. COCO annotators sometimes skip objects, so treat a surprising Extra box as a judgement call to discuss, not always a mistake.

      Photos: COCO dataset (cocodataset.org), originally from Flickr under Creative Commons licenses. Labels: COCO Consortium, CC BY 4.0.

      Your own BDD100K photos

      Real dashcam photos are scored against the labels Berkeley's annotators made. A few rules change, which is normal: every labeling project has its own guide.

      1. Visible part only. BDD100K boxes cover only the part of an object you can see. For a hidden object, box what is visible and tick Occluded.
      2. Cyclist covers bicycle and motorcycle riders, box rider and vehicle together. Parked bikes are optional.
      3. Signs are not scored. BDD100K groups every sign together, so stop sign boxes are allowed but never required.
      4. Vehicle includes cars, trucks, buses and trains.

      To get the data: create a free account at bdd-data.berkeley.edu, accept the license, then download the 100K images and the detection labels. Use a handful of photos from the val folder with the val label file.
